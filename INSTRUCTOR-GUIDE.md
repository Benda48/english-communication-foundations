# Instructor Guide
## Best Practices & Professional Support

---

## A. Best Practices for Teaching

### 1. Use English from Minute One
- Greet learners in English at the door
- Explain instructions in simple English, then gesture
- Write key words on the board
- Never apologize for using English
- Learners will understand context and tone

**Example:**
"Hello! Welcome! Please sit down. [gesture to seat] Today we learn to introduce ourselves. [mime introduction] Okay? Good!"

### 2. Vary Activities Every 8-12 Minutes

**Why?** Adult attention spans peak at 10-12 minutes. After that, engagement drops sharply.

**Solution:** Move between activity types:
- Frontal presentation (10 min max)
- Pair work (10 min)
- Group game (10 min)
- Individual writing (5 min)
- Whole class discussion (8 min)

### 3. Ensure Every Learner Speaks at Least Once Per Session

**Tracking Sheet:** Use participation tracker (see Annexes)

**Target Silent Learners:**
- Ask easy, predictable questions: "Maria, what's your name?"
- Give preparation time: "Think about this for 30 seconds, then tell your partner"
- Use pair work instead of whole-class: less intimidating
- Offer sentence starters: "My favorite food is..."

### 4. Prepare a Backup Activity

**You need:** One or two additional 5-10 minute activities in your back pocket for:
- Class finishing early
- Technical issues
- Low energy
- Group needing extra practice

**Simple backup activities:**
- Pictionary with vocabulary
- 20 Questions game
- Bingo with numbers, colors, or vocabulary
- Speed dating (rapid pair rotations)

### 5. Always Finish On Time

**Why?** Adult learners have work, family, other commitments. Respecting time builds trust.

**Strategy:**
- Have a 55-minute alarm
- Last 5 minutes: devoirs explanation + goodbye
- Finish EXACTLY at 60 minutes

## B. Motivation Strategies

### 1. Weekly Progression Display

**What:** Post the "Can-Do statements" achieved this week on the classroom wall.

**Example:**
"✓ THIS WEEK WE CAN:
- Introduce ourselves
- Spell our names
- Understand simple questions"

**Effect:** Learners see tangible progress.

### 2. Learner of the Week Recognition

**Criteria:** NOT perfection, but:
- Effort and participation
- Risk-taking and trying new things
- Helping a classmate
- Consistent attendance
- Showing courage to speak

**How:** Announce in class, mention in WhatsApp group, take a photo.

### 3. Collective Challenges

**Example:**
"If 25 of you post a voice message this week, I'll bring pastries next week!"

**Effect:** Creates group motivation and accountability.

### 4. Individual Learner Journals

**At the end of each session:**
Each learner writes ONE sentence:
"Today I learned..."

**Collect weekly, give written feedback.**

**Effect:** Reflection deepens learning and shows you care.

### 5. Personal Messages to Absent Learners

**On WhatsApp:**
"Hi Kofi! We missed you in class today. The homework is... See you next class!"

**Effect:** Shows that absences are noticed and valued.

## C. Classroom Management (30 Learners)

### Group Rotation System: Color Cards

**Setup:**
- 5 different colors
- 6 learners per color
- Assign a color to each learner on Day 1

**Rotations:**

| Configuration | When to Use | Duration |
|---------------|-------------|----------|
| **Pairs, same color** | Regular pair work, information gap | 10-12 min |
| **Pairs, different colors** | Peer learning, mixed level practice | 10-12 min |
| **Groups of 4-5, mixed** | Collaborative tasks, debates, preparation | 12-15 min |
| **Demi-classe (15)** | Mingle activities, circulation games | 8-10 min |
| **Full class** | Presentations, listening, whole-class games | 5-8 min |

### Participation Tracking

**Use a checklist:**

| Learner | S1 | S2 | S3 | S4 | S5 | S6 | S7 | S8 |
|---------|----|----|----|----|----|----|----|----|  
| Ama | ✓ | ✓✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓✓ |
| Kofi | — | ✓ | ✓ | ○ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |

**Legend:**
- ✓ = spoke at least once
- ✓✓ = active participation
- — = absent
- ○ = present but silent (target this learner next session)

## D. Error Correction Techniques

### When to Correct

| Error Type | When | How |
|-----------|------|-----|
| Blocks comprehension | Immediately | Explicit correction |
| Recurring pattern | After task | Explicit teaching moment |
| Minor during fluency | Not at all | Implicit reformulation only |
| During practice drill | Immediately | Direct feedback |
| During peer work | Note it | Delayed correction after |

### Error Correction in Practice

**Scenario 1: Student says "I go to home" during pair work**

**Option A (Implicit Reformulation):**
"Oh nice! You go home! What time?"

**Option B (Self-Correction):**
"Can you correct that?"
[Student: "I go home."]
"Perfect!"

**Option C (Explicit):**
"Attention, we say I GO HOME, not I go TO home. No 'to' before home."

## E. Feedback Framework

### The "Sandwich" Model

```
[POSITIVE]  "Your pronunciation was very clear!"
    ↓
[IMPROVEMENT]  "Next time, focus on the /θ/ sound in 'three'."
    ↓
[ENCOURAGEMENT]  "You're making great progress. Keep going!"
```

### Never Say:
- "That's wrong."
- "You made a mistake."
- "No, no, no."

### Instead Say:
- "Good try! Listen to this..."
- "Almost! Let me show you..."
- "Very close! One more time..."

## F. Building a Positive Learning Culture

### From Day 1:

1. **Learn all 30 names by Lesson 3**
   - Use name + praise: "Excellent, Maria!"
   - Learners feel recognized

2. **Create a "no judgment" zone**
   - "Errors are proof you're learning"
   - "Shy is okay, but try anyway"
   - You model risk-taking too

3. **Use humor**
   - Make mistakes on purpose
   - Laugh at yourself
   - Light-hearted games

4. **Celebrate effort**
   - "I love that you tried!"
   - "Your courage is amazing!"
   - Recognition > perfection

5. **Build peer support**
   - Partner strong with weaker learners
   - "Help your partner!"
   - Peer praise: "Did everyone hear how clear Kofi's pronunciation was?"

## G. Common Challenges & Solutions

### Challenge 1: One Learner Dominates

**Solution:**
- Pair dominant learner with shy learner
- Ask specific questions to others: "Maria, what do you think?"
- Use turn-taking structures: "You speak first, you listen. Then switch."
- Gently: "Let's hear from someone who hasn't spoken yet."

### Challenge 2: Learners Speaking French in Pair Work

**Solution:**
- "English only, please! I don't speak French. 😊"
- Model the activity FIRST with a learner
- Provide sentence starters
- Award participation: "I heard excellent English from this table!"

### Challenge 3: Learners Forget Words

**Solution:**
- "That's okay! Use gestures or draw it."
- "Ask a friend."
- Provide vocabulary list on wall
- "We call this... [give word]"
- Normalize: "Real English speakers forget words too!"

### Challenge 4: Learners Anxious About Mistakes

**Solution:**
- Model mistakes yourself: "I say... wait, that's wrong. I say..."
- Celebrate errors in class: "Great try! That's how we learn!"
- Individual feedback, not public correction
- Explain CECRL A1: "Mistakes are normal at this level."

### Challenge 5: Class Energy Low

**Solution:**
- Stand up activity (Mingle, Information Gap)
- Music + movement
- Competition (teams, races)
- Change pairs/groups
- Short game

## I. Self-Reflection Questions

Every week, ask yourself:

1. **Did every learner speak at least once?** ✓
2. **Was energy high? Did learners enjoy class?** ✓
3. **Did I vary activities?** ✓
4. **Did I use English throughout?** ✓
5. **Did I finish on time?** ✓
6. **Did I give positive feedback?** ✓
7. **Which learner needs more support next week?** ✓
8. **What went well?** ✓
9. **What will I improve next time?** ✓
