# Pedagogical Framework

## Core Philosophy

**"You learn to speak by speaking."**

This curriculum rests on a fundamental conviction: grammar is not an end in itself — it is a tool at the service of communication. Each session is designed so that the learner speaks from the first minutes, makes mistakes safely, corrects them progressively, and gains confidence.

---

## Teaching Methodologies

### 1. Communicative Language Teaching (CLT)

CLT places real communication at the heart of learning. The learner does not learn the language to speak in class — they learn to use it in authentic situations.

**Key Principle:** Form (grammar) is at the service of meaning (communication). Not all errors are corrected — only those that hinder comprehension.

**Activities in CLT:**
- Information gaps (learner has incomplete information and must ask to complete it)
- Real-world tasks (ordering food, asking directions, introductions)
- Problem-solving activities
- Games with genuine communication need

---

### 2. Task-Based Learning (TBL)

Each session is organized around a communicative task to accomplish: introducing yourself to a new colleague, ordering a meal, asking directions. Grammar and vocabulary are introduced in service of this task, never abstractly.

**TBL Phase Sequence:**

| Phase | Duration | What Happens |
|-------|----------|---------------|
| **Pre-Task** | 10 min | Activation of prior knowledge, presentation of key vocabulary, context |
| **Task** | 20 min | Learners accomplish the task in pairs or groups |
| **Planning** | 5 min | Learners prepare their oral or written report |
| **Report** | 10 min | Restitution in front of class, teacher feedback |
| **Linguistic Analysis** | 10 min | Teacher highlights useful forms used in the task |
| **Practice** | 5 min | Targeted exercises on identified forms |

---

### 3. PPP Model (Presentation – Practice – Production)

For introducing new linguistic elements (grammar, vocabulary, expressions):

- **Presentation:** Teacher presents new form in context with visual or oral examples
- **Controlled Practice:** Learners practice with guided exercises (repetition, substitution, drills)
- **Free Production:** Learners use the new form in open communicative activities

---

### 4. Andragogy (Adult Learning Principles)

| Principle | Application in This Program |
|-----------|------------------------------|
| **Need to know** | Each activity begins with "Why you need this in real life" |
| **Prior experience** | Activities build on what learners already know |
| **Task orientation** | Objectives are formulated as "What you can DO" |
| **Internal motivation** | Program chooses themes linked to learners' personal goals |
| **Active involvement** | Learners participate actively from hour one |

## Francophone-Specific Challenges & Solutions

### 1. Non-Existent Sounds

| Challenge | Explanation | Solution |
|-----------|-------------|----------|
| **/θ/ sound (think, three)** | Doesn't exist in French — often replaced by /s/ or /t/ | Specific drills: tongue between teeth, mirror exercises |
| **/ð/ sound (this, that)** | Doesn't exist in French — often replaced by /z/ or /d/ | Same as above + minimal pairs (this/zis) |
| **/h/ aspiration (hello, have)** | French H is silent; English H is aspirated | Hand-in-front-of-mouth drill to feel air flow |
| **/w/ vs /v/ confusion** | Common: "vater" instead of "water" | Minimal pairs: wine/vine, west/vest |

### 2. Word Stress (Prosody)

**The Problem:** French is syllabic (all syllables have similar weight). English is stress-timed (one syllable is prominent).

**Examples of Errors:**
- French-influenced: ho-TEL (equal syllables) → English: hoTEL (strong stress on second)
- French-influenced: STU-dent (stress on first) → English: STUdent (stress on first, but compressed)

**Solution:** Mark accent stress in CAPITALS on the board. Learners clap or tap on the stressed syllable.

### 3. Faux Amis (False Friends)

| English Word | Francophone Error | Correct Use |
|--------------|-------------------|-------------|
| **Actually** | Means "currently" | Means "in fact" or "in reality" |
| **Eventually** | Means "possibly/maybe" | Means "in the end/finally" |
| **Sensible** | Means "sensitive" | Means "reasonable/wise" |
| **Embarrassed** | Means "pregnant" | Means "ashamed" |
| **Fabric** | Means "factory" | Means "material/cloth" |

**Solution:** Dedicated faux amis list distributed in Week 1, reviewed weekly.

### 4. Shyness About Oral Production

**The Problem:** French school culture emphasizes written perfection. Oral errors = shame.

**Solution:**
- Create a non-judgmental, error-welcoming environment
- Value risk-taking over perfection
- Use peer support and pair work to reduce anxiety
- Celebrate effort: "Good try! Almost! Listen..."

### 5. French Syntactic Interference

| French Pattern | English Equivalent | Correct English |
|---------------|--------------------|------------------|
| "J'ai 20 ans" (I have 20 years) | I have 20 years | I am 20 years old |
| "C'est possible?" (It's possible?) | Is it possible? | Is that possible? |
| "On va?" (We go?) | Shall we go? | Shall we go? |

**Solution:** Contrastive exercises explicitly comparing French and English structures.

## Core Teaching Principles

### 1. Speech First
- Prioritize oral expression from Day 1
- Writing is secondary
- Listening comes before speaking
- Reading comes after speaking is established

### 2. Error as Learning Tool
- Errors are not faults — they are evidence of learning
- Correction should be strategic, not constant
- Self-correction is preferable to teacher correction
- Some errors are left alone if communication is clear

### 3. Immediate Applicability
- Every hour should produce at least ONE thing learners can use immediately
- "You can go home and use this today"
- Vocabulary should be functional, not random
- Role plays and real situations dominate

### 4. Pleasure & Motivation
- Enjoyment is not optional — it's essential
- Variety prevents boredom
- Humor and personality of the teacher matter
- Peer support and friendly competition motivate

## Session Design Principles

### Activity Rotation (No activity > 12 minutes)

- **0-8 min:** Warm-up (energetic, low-pressure)
- **8-16 min:** Vocabulary (visual, kinesthetic, repetition)
- **16-21 min:** Pronunciation (specific weekly focus)
- **21-28 min:** Grammar in context (micro-lessons)
- **28-38 min:** Pair work (controlled communication)
- **38-48 min:** Group work (semi-free communication)
- **48-55 min:** Listening or reading (receptive skill)
- **55-60 min:** Writing & homework (wrap-up)

### Every Learner Speaks At Least Once Per Session

- Track participation with a checklist
- Target silent learners with kind, direct questions
- Use pair work to ensure everyone has speaking time
- Never allow one dominant learner to monopolize class time

## Correction Strategies

### 1. Implicit Reformulation (During Fluency Tasks)

**Learner:** "I go to home."
**Teacher:** "Oh, you go home! What time?"

*Effect:* Learner hears the correct form without interruption to flow.

### 2. Self-Correction Solicitation

**Learner:** "I go to home."
**Teacher:** "Can you correct that?"
**Learner:** "I go home."

*Effect:* Learner actively corrects, reinforcing learning.

### 3. Explicit Correction (Recurring Errors)

**Used for:** Errors that block comprehension or appear repeatedly

**Format:** "Attention, we say I GO HOME, not I go TO home."

### 4. Delayed Correction

**Used for:** Free production activities
**Timing:** After activity ends, not during
**Effect:* Maintains fluency during speaking task

### 5. Peer Correction

**Teacher:** "Can someone help [name]?"

*Effect:* Builds community and peer support.

## Feedback Framework ("Sandwich" Model)

**Structure:**
1. **Positive point:** What was good
2. **Point to improve:** What needs work
3. **Positive point:** Encouragement to continue

**Example:**
"Maria, I loved your confidence and energy! Next time, let's focus on the /θ/ sound in 'think' — you said 'sink.' You've got great communication skills — keep it up!"

---

This framework ensures that teaching is learner-centered, communicative, and culturally sensitive to francophone adult learners.
